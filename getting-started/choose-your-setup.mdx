Jurni connects to the commerce setup your brand already uses. **Shopify is supported through a native integration.** Headless storefronts, custom storefronts, WooCommerce, Salesforce, BigCommerce, and other platforms require the connection appropriate to that stack.

This page explains compatibility. It is not an onboarding step where you choose a new commerce platform.

## Shopify: native integration

For a Shopify brand, connect the existing store through Jurni's Shopify authorization flow during onboarding. The native integration provides the connection to Shopify commerce; you then configure the features you need, such as products, Jurni Core, and publishing.

For a store using a Shopify Online Store theme:

- Activate [Jurni Core](/getting-started/activating-jurni-core) in the active theme.
- Work with the connected products and their available purchase options in Jurni.
- Publish using a Jurni URL on your connected subdomain, or the Shopify app-proxy route while no custom Jurni subdomain is configured.
- Optionally configure [Shopify Pages](/shopify-pages/setup-publish) to publish inside a Shopify page template.

**Native integration does not mean every third-party Shopify app automatically runs on a Jurni-hosted page.** Tracking, reviews, forms, cart drawers, and other widgets still need the appropriate integration and testing.

## Shopify with a headless storefront

A headless Shopify brand still uses Shopify for commerce, but shoppers browse a custom frontend rather than a standard Shopify theme.

The Shopify connection and the custom frontend's tracking are different parts of this setup. Enabling an app embed in a Shopify theme does not install that script on the separate frontend. Your developer and the Jurni team need to confirm the frontend script installation, cart continuity, checkout handoff, and purchase attribution.

See [Shopify headless setup](/getting-started/shopify-headless). Native Shopify Pages instructions apply to Shopify's theme-rendered pages, not automatically to a headless frontend.

## WooCommerce, Salesforce, BigCommerce, and custom storefronts

Jurni can work with these setups through an implementation agreed with your team. During onboarding, identify your existing provider. The current non-Shopify onboarding flow includes a team-assisted handoff rather than the native Shopify authorization path.

Before launch, agree how these four parts work:

| Part | What needs to be confirmed |
| --- | --- |
| **Product data** | Where Jurni receives products, variants, prices, availability, and any subscription options. |
| **Cart and checkout** | What each purchasing action does and where the shopper completes the order. |
| **Tracking** | Where Jurni and third-party scripts run, including the downstream storefront and consent requirements. |
| **Purchase measurement** | Which event or backend integration supplies completed purchases and revenue. |

A working landing-page URL alone does not confirm that the purchase or revenue integration is complete. See [Non-Shopify setup](/getting-started/setup-non-shopify).

## Publishing follows the storefront environment

| Existing environment | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| **Shopify Online Store theme** | Native Shopify connection; Jurni app-proxy/custom-subdomain URLs; optional native Shopify Pages. |
| **Shopify headless** | Shopify commerce plus a separate frontend implementation. Confirm tracking and commerce handoffs with your developer. |
| **Other commerce platforms** | Assisted/custom connection for the provider and storefront you already use. Do not apply Shopify app-proxy or Shopify Pages instructions. |

For standard Shopify stores, `brand.com/apps/jurni/<handle>` and `brand.com/pages/<handle>` are different publishing routes. A [Smart Link](/building-funnels/smart-links) is the traffic-routing layer, not a different commerce platform.
