# Build a founder-story landing page

Use a founder-story page when the founder's **experience, point of view, or reason for building the product** is genuinely part of why a customer should believe in it.

The story is not a biography. Its job is to make the product's point of view understandable and credible.

## When to use it

A founder-led page is especially useful when:

- the product was created after a meaningful firsthand problem
- the category has a trust gap
- formulation, sourcing, design, or product philosophy needs explanation
- founder credibility is already important in ads or creator content
- the brand competes on a clear belief about how the problem should be solved

Do not force the format when the founder story has little connection to the customer's purchase decision.

## Step 1: collect the real founder material

Use first-party, approved source material where possible:

- founder interview or transcript
- approved founder letter
- brand origin brief
- product-development story
- founder video transcript
- exact approved quotes

Then extract:

```text
CUSTOMER PROBLEM
[the problem the story connects to]

FOUNDER EXPERIENCE / OBSERVATION
[what actually happened]

WHAT FELT MISSING
[the gap in existing options]

PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
[the belief or design principle that followed]

WHAT WAS BUILT DIFFERENTLY
[approved product truth]

WHY THE CUSTOMER SHOULD CARE
[customer relevance]
```

If you cannot fill these in with real source material, gather more context before generating the story.

## Step 2: make the customer the reason the story matters

A strong founder page often follows:

1. **Customer-facing hook** — the problem or belief.
2. **Founder connection** — why this became personal or important.
3. **The gap** — what was unsatisfying about existing approaches.
4. **The principle** — what the founder believed should be different.
5. **The product** — how that principle shaped the product.
6. **Proof** — evidence the product deserves trust.
7. **Purchase decision** — what the customer can do now.

The founder can be the narrator without becoming the subject of every section.

## Copy-ready Jurni prompt

```text
Build a founder-story landing page for [audience].

SOURCES
Use the attached founder transcript / approved founder copy as the only source for founder facts and quotes.
Use the attached product and AI Knowledge for product and brand information.

STORY ARC
1. Open with the customer problem or belief: [problem].
2. Introduce the founder's real connection to it: [approved summary].
3. Explain what the founder believed was missing: [gap].
4. Explain the product principle that followed: [principle].
5. Introduce [product] as the result of that thinking.
6. Add approved proof and product details.
7. Transition clearly into the purchase section and FAQ.

VOICE
Personal, specific, and credible. Preserve exact founder quotes where supplied.
Avoid making the founder sound like generic brand copy.

DO NOT
- invent founder quotes, credentials, personal events, timelines, or motivations
- invent research or product claims
- overstate what competitors do
- add fake “from my kitchen to millions” style milestones

GOAL
Help the reader understand why the product exists and why that matters to them—not simply learn about the founder.
```

## Step 3: use exact quotes carefully

If the founder said something especially useful, preserve it as an actual quote. Do not ask AI to make a paraphrase look like a direct quote.

Label source material clearly:

```text
EXACT QUOTE — preserve verbatim:
“[approved founder quote]”

BACKGROUND — can be paraphrased:
[approved context]
```

## Step 4: connect product details to the principle

The strongest founder pages show how a belief affected the product.

For example:

- “We wanted fewer steps” → product format or routine design
- “We couldn't find X without Y” → approved formulation choice
- “We wanted people to know exactly what they were buying” → sourcing/transparency detail

Do not create a principle after the fact to make every feature sound meaningful.

## Step 5: keep the story visually human

Useful assets can include:

- founder image
- product-development imagery
- handwritten or archival material the brand owns
- product closeups
- simple founder quote treatment

Avoid filling the page with generic stock imagery that breaks the authenticity of the story.

## Step 6: introduce proof beyond the founder

The founder can explain **why the product exists**. Customers still need reasons to believe the product works for their decision.

Use approved customer proof, reviews, product evidence, or other trust signals where appropriate. The founder's confidence alone is not independent proof.

## Step 7: review for biography drift

```text
Review this founder-story page.
Do not edit yet.

For each section, answer:
“What does this help the customer understand or believe about their purchase decision?”

Flag any section that is mainly biography, company history, or founder praise without customer relevance.
Flag any founder fact or direct quote that is not clearly supported by the supplied source material.
```

## What good looks like

The visitor should leave with a clearer understanding of the brand's **point of view** and how that point of view shaped the product.

The story should feel difficult for another brand to copy because it is grounded in real people, decisions, and product truth.

## Common mistakes

**Opening with “Meet our founder.”** Lead with the customer tension unless the founder is already the traffic hook.

**Invented founder voice.** AI can shape structure and transitions; it should not manufacture personal history or quotes.

**Founder as the only proof.** Add relevant customer or product evidence.

**Too much chronology.** Dates and milestones matter only if they support the buying story.

**No clear conversion transition.** The story should eventually make product evaluation easier.

## What to test next

- customer-problem opening versus founder-first opening
- founder video/quote near hero versus later
- shorter founder section embedded in a PDP versus full founder-story page
- product introduced earlier versus after the core story insight
