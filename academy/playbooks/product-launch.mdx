Use this playbook when you are launching a new product, new formula, new format, or materially new collection and need a dedicated page that explains **why the launch matters to the customer**.

A launch page should not rely on “new” as the whole reason to buy. “New” creates attention; the page still has to explain relevance, differentiation, proof, and the next action.

## When to use it

Good use cases:

- new product or SKU;
- new product category for an existing brand;
- meaningful reformulation / format change;
- limited launch to a specific audience;
- paid campaign where the homepage or normal PDP cannot tell the launch story clearly.

If the main story is purely a temporary discount, use the [campaign & offer prompts](/academy/prompts/campaigns-offers) rather than forcing every sale into a product-launch narrative.

## Before you start

Gather:

- connected product and actual purchase options;
- approved launch positioning;
- approved claims / product facts;
- product imagery / launch creative;
- founder / R&D / customer story only when real and relevant;
- proof available at launch;
- launch offer and dates, if real;
- traffic source and audience.

Answer these questions:

1. What customer problem makes this launch relevant?
2. What is genuinely new or different?
3. Why would the audience care now?
4. What proof can we use today?
5. What is the intended purchase action?

## Phase 1 — Choose the launch story

Common launch stories include:

- **new solution to an existing problem**;
- **new format / easier way to use the product**;
- **new benefit or experience** supported by approved facts;
- **customer-requested expansion** when you have real evidence;
- **product ecosystem / collection expansion**.

Avoid vague launch language such as “the future is here” unless it is followed immediately by something concrete.

## Phase 2 — Build the first version

```text
Build a launch landing page for [PRODUCT].

AUDIENCE
[AUDIENCE]

TRAFFIC SOURCE
[CHANNEL / CAMPAIGN]

LAUNCH STORY
[WHY THIS PRODUCT IS RELEVANT / WHAT IS NEW]

USE
- attached product for real product and purchase information;
- launch assets for imagery;
- AI Knowledge for approved claims and brand voice;
- supplied proof only.

PAGE FLOW
1. Launch hero: what is new and why this audience should care
2. Fast credibility / proof
3. Customer problem or use case
4. What makes the product different
5. Product details / how it fits into the routine
6. Approved social proof / evidence
7. Product / purchase section
8. FAQ about the product and launch
9. Final CTA

Do not invent launch dates, scarcity, early-bird pricing, reviews, claims, or guarantees.
If there is no approved discount, do not create one.
Prioritize mobile.
```

## Phase 3 — Make the launch feel specific

Check whether the page could belong to any brand if you swapped the logo.

A specific launch page uses:

- real product form / packaging;
- the actual customer use case;
- a concrete newness statement;
- proof tied to the product;
- language from the campaign, founder, or audience when approved.

A generic launch page leans on adjectives: “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “premium,” “innovative.” Replace adjectives with the actual reason the product is different.

## Phase 4 — Handle proof realistically

New products often have less product-specific proof at launch. Do not compensate by fabricating it.

Possible legitimate proof sources:

- brand trust / existing customer base if accurate;
- approved founder / formulation rationale;
- product facts / certifications;
- relevant brand reviews clearly presented as brand-level rather than this exact new product;
- pre-launch testers only when their feedback is real and approved.

If product-specific review widgets have little data, design the page around the evidence you actually have rather than creating fake review density.

## Phase 5 — Explain the purchase decision

A launch can introduce unfamiliar choices. Make sure shoppers understand:

- product / format;
- variant / size;
- one-time vs subscription where offered;
- bundle / offer only when actually configured;
- shipping / guarantee details from approved sources.

The launch story should lead naturally to the purchase section rather than ending abruptly with “Buy Now.”

## Phase 6 — Decide whether urgency is real

Only use urgency that is factually true and operationally supported:

- real launch date;
- real limited edition;
- real inventory constraint if your team explicitly approves communicating it;
- real promotional end date.

Do not generate countdowns, “selling fast,” “almost gone,” or invented launch windows because they are common ecommerce patterns.

## Phase 7 — QA the campaign

Test:

- the final launch URL / Smart Link;
- product and variant;
- purchase option;
- offer if applicable;
- mobile hero and product imagery;
- page metadata / sharing preview if the launch will be shared;
- tracking / consent;
- checkout.

Use [Launch your first campaign](/academy/first-campaign) for the full campaign workflow.

## What good looks like

After the first 20–30 seconds, a visitor should be able to explain:

> “This is new because **X**, it matters to me because **Y**, and I can buy / try it by **Z**.”

If the only takeaway is “this brand launched something new,” the page needs a stronger customer story.

## Common mistakes

### Making “new” the benefit

New is a context cue, not necessarily customer value.

### Overloading the page with founder / R&D story

Use it where it builds belief. The customer remains the protagonist.

### Inventing launch social proof

Use real evidence or explain the product without it.

### Hiding the actual product choice below a long story

Long-form education can work, but the shopper should still understand what they are eventually buying.

### Creating urgency that the business cannot support

False scarcity is both low-trust and operationally risky.

## Useful follow-up tests

- launch story: problem-led vs newness-led;
- product-first vs founder-first hero;
- technical differentiation vs customer outcome framing;
- proof placement;
- shorter vs longer launch education for the same audience.
