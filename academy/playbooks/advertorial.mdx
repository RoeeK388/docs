# Build an advertorial landing page

Use an advertorial when the visitor needs to **understand or believe something before the product makes sense**. The format works best when education is part of the conversion job—not because an editorial layout is fashionable.

A strong advertorial feels like useful reading that naturally arrives at a product decision. It should not pretend to be independent journalism when it is a brand-owned campaign page.

## When to use an advertorial

Consider this format when:

- the category or mechanism is unfamiliar
- the audience is problem-aware but not yet product-aware
- an important misconception needs to be reframed
- the founder or product insight creates a compelling “why now”
- the product needs more context than a standard PDP gives it

Use a more direct page when traffic is already highly product-aware and primarily needs price, proof, or purchase choices.

## Step 1: define the editorial thesis

An advertorial needs one central idea. Fill in:

```text
AUDIENCE
[who is reading]

PROBLEM / DESIRE
[what matters now]

OLD BELIEF
[what they currently assume]

NEW INSIGHT
[what the article should help them understand]

WHY PRODUCT BECOMES RELEVANT
[the logical bridge]

PRIMARY ACTION
[what the reader should do next]
```

If the “new insight” is simply “our product is great,” you do not yet have an advertorial thesis.

## Step 2: structure the story

A useful starting flow is:

1. **Editorial headline** — open with the problem, tension, or insight.
2. **Recognition** — show the reader you understand the situation.
3. **Why common approaches disappoint** — only where you have a fair, supportable basis.
4. **The insight** — explain the mechanism, principle, or new way to think about the problem.
5. **Product introduction** — show why your product follows from that insight.
6. **Proof** — approved evidence, reviews, founder context, or relevant product facts.
7. **Purchase transition** — move clearly from reading to evaluating the offer.
8. **FAQ / objections** — answer remaining purchase questions.

Not every page needs every section. Remove anything that repeats the same argument.

## Copy-ready Jurni prompt

```text
CONTEXT
Build an advertorial-style landing page for [audience] arriving from [traffic source].
Campaign angle: [angle].

EDITORIAL THESIS
The reader currently believes: [old belief].
The page should help them understand: [new insight].
This makes [product] relevant because: [approved reason].

SOURCES
Use the attached product, approved brief / research, campaign creative, AI Knowledge, and approved proof.

FLOW
1. Editorial-style headline focused on the reader's problem or insight.
2. Recognition of the situation in the reader's language.
3. Explanation of why [approved limitation / misconception] matters.
4. Clear explanation of the new insight.
5. Natural introduction of the product.
6. Approved proof.
7. Product / purchase section.
8. FAQ for the supplied objections.

STYLE
Readable, specific, and editorial. Use short paragraphs, meaningful subheads, and visual breaks.
Do not make the opening read like a product catalog.

DO NOT
- pretend this is independent journalism
- invent data, experts, studies, reviews, or founder quotes
- make unsupported comparison claims
- invent pricing, savings, deadlines, or guarantees
```

## Step 3: make the opening earn the next scroll

An advertorial headline should create relevance, not curiosity at any cost.

Weak:

> The Secret Everyone Is Talking About

Stronger:

> Why [audience] are rethinking [specific old approach]

The second version tells the reader what the article is actually about.

The first two sections should make the visitor feel: **“This is describing my problem accurately.”** Only then does the product introduction have context.

## Step 4: keep the product reveal logical

The product should arrive because the reader has learned something—not because the page suddenly switches modes.

A useful bridge sounds like:

- “That is the principle behind…”
- “This is why we designed…”
- “Instead of [old approach], the product is built around…”

Avoid an abrupt transition from educational copy to “BUY NOW.”

## Step 5: use proof where skepticism appears

As the article makes bigger claims, the reader's need for proof grows.

Match proof to the claim:

- customer quote near the lived experience it supports
- product detail near a mechanism claim
- approved review rating near purchase confidence
- founder context near the origin or design principle
- comparison evidence near a comparison

Do not use decorative proof badges that do not support the specific argument.

## Step 6: make the purchase transition unmistakable

An editorial format can become so subtle that readers do not know what to do next.

When you introduce the product, clearly show:

- what the product is
- why it fits the insight
- the approved offer or purchase choices
- the primary CTA

The page can feel editorial without hiding that it is a commercial experience.

## Step 7: review for “AI article” symptoms

Use this cleanup prompt:

```text
Review this advertorial for generic AI writing.
Do not change factual claims.

Flag:
- vague opening sentences
- repeated benefit language
- unsupported dramatic claims
- filler transitions
- generic “in today's world” style language
- paragraphs that do not advance the argument

For each, suggest a tighter rewrite grounded in the supplied audience, product, and approved source material.
```

## What good looks like

A good advertorial should have a clear argument you can summarize in one sentence. Each section should either build understanding, reduce skepticism, introduce relevant proof, or move toward the purchase decision.

The reader should never have to wonder why a section exists.

## Common mistakes

**Starting with the product too soon.** Then the page is just a PDP wearing an editorial design.

**Using fake editorial authority.** Do not invent publications, experts, “investigations,” or independent endorsements.

**Stretching weak material into a long article.** Length should follow the amount of education needed.

**Making the transition to product feel like a bait-and-switch.** Be transparent and logical.

**Repeating one benefit eight ways.** A longer page needs progression, not repetition.

## What to test next

Useful advertorial hypotheses include:

- problem-led headline versus insight-led headline
- founder/brand origin introduced earlier versus later
- product introduced after one education section versus after several
- proof embedded throughout versus concentrated near product
- long educational path versus condensed version for warmer traffic
