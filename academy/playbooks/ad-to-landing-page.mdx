Use this playbook when a paid ad is doing the job of earning the click, but the destination is too generic to continue the same story.

The goal is **message match**: the visitor should immediately feel that the page they landed on belongs to the ad they just clicked.

## When to use this playbook

Use it when:

- a strong Meta / TikTok creative points to a generic PDP or homepage;
- different ad angles all land on the same page;
- the landing page headline does not reflect the ad promise;
- a creator ad has strong language or proof that disappears after the click;
- you want a dedicated page before running an A/B test.

Do not use it to blindly copy the ad into a webpage. An ad earns attention; a landing page has to build enough understanding and trust for the next action.

## Before you start

Gather:

- the exact ad creative or a representative screenshot/video;
- the campaign audience and traffic source;
- the connected product;
- the approved offer, if any;
- approved reviews / UGC / proof;
- brand guidance / AI Knowledge;
- the real purchase path.

Write down one sentence:

> The ad gets attention by saying **[ANGLE]** to **[AUDIENCE]**. The page must help that visitor believe **[WHAT THEY NEED TO BELIEVE NEXT]** before taking **[ACTION]**.

That sentence is the strategy for the page.

## Phase 1 — Decode the ad

Before building, ask Jurni to identify what actually needs to carry through.

```text
Do not build yet.
Analyze the attached ad as a landing-page brief.

Identify:
1. Audience being spoken to
2. Core promise / angle
3. Product or use case emphasized
4. Tone and customer language
5. Proof or credibility used in the ad
6. Offer / CTA expectation
7. What the landing page must explain next

Separate what is directly present in the ad from any assumptions.
Do not invent customer research or claims.
```

Review this output. If Jurni misunderstands the ad angle, correct it before building.

## Phase 2 — Decide what the first screen must do

The first screen should usually preserve four things from the ad:

- **message** — same core reason to care;
- **product** — no bait-and-switch in what is emphasized;
- **visual expectation** — enough continuity that the page feels related;
- **next action** — the CTA should make sense after the ad click.

A good first screen is not necessarily identical to the ad. It should answer what the ad did not have room to answer.

Example:

- Ad: “My 2-minute morning routine when I have no time.”
- Page: “A simpler 2-minute morning routine” + what the product is + approved proof + product visual + clear purchase CTA.

## Phase 3 — Build the page around the ad angle

```text
Build a dedicated landing page for the attached ad.

AUDIENCE
[AUDIENCE]

AD ANGLE
[ANGLE]

PRIMARY ACTION
[ACTION]

USE
- attached ad for message and creative direction;
- attached product for real product information;
- AI Knowledge for approved brand voice and claims;
- supplied reviews / proof only.

PAGE JOB
The hero should feel like a direct continuation of the ad.
Then explain only what this visitor needs in order to understand, trust,
and act on the product.

Suggested flow:
1. Message-matched hero
2. Fast proof / trust
3. Benefits tied to the ad angle
4. Product / mechanism education
5. Approved social proof
6. Purchase section
7. Objection-handling FAQ
8. Final CTA

Do not invent claims, reviews, prices, discounts, or urgency.
Prioritize mobile.
```

Do not polish every section yet. First decide whether the **story** is coherent from ad → hero → proof → product → purchase.

## Phase 4 — Check for message drift

Use this review prompt:

```text
Do not edit yet.
Compare the attached ad with the current landing page.

Check for mismatch in:
- headline promise;
- audience language;
- product emphasis;
- visual emphasis;
- offer;
- CTA expectation;
- proof.

For each mismatch, tell me whether it is:
1. A real inconsistency we should fix
2. Additional page context that is reasonable after the click
3. An assumption that needs human review
```

You are not trying to make the whole page repeat the ad. You are trying to prevent the visitor from having to mentally start over.

## Phase 5 — Strengthen the decision journey

Once message match is strong, improve the page based on what the visitor still needs.

Ask:

- Does the visitor know what the product actually is?
- Is the key benefit grounded in an approved product fact?
- Does useful proof appear before a high-friction purchase ask?
- Are you repeating the same benefit in five sections?
- Is the purchase section clear enough for the intended traffic temperature?
- Are objections answered where they become relevant?

Cold traffic typically needs more context than returning customers. That does not mean “make it long”; it means **earn the next decision**.

## Phase 6 — Mobile pass

Paid-social traffic is often mobile-heavy, so deliberately check the first screen and purchase path.

```text
Review mobile only.

For the first screen:
- keep the ad-matched headline immediately visible;
- make the product recognizable;
- keep the primary CTA easy to reach;
- reduce decorative spacing that delays the decision;
- avoid cropping the key product / creator visual;
- no horizontal overflow.

Keep desktop unchanged unless a shared edit is required.
```

## Phase 7 — Test the actual campaign path

Open the exact link you plan to use in the ad.

Verify:

- the ad link reaches the intended page;
- the campaign parameters are preserved as expected;
- the correct product / offer is shown;
- CTA and purchase behavior work;
- mobile rendering matches what you reviewed;
- consent and tracking behave as expected;
- checkout continues the campaign correctly.

Use [Check your campaign link](/publishing/check-campaign-link) for the operational checklist.

## What good looks like

A strong ad-matched page should pass a five-second test:

- **I know I am in the right place.**
- **The product / solution is recognizable.**
- **The page is talking about the same thing as the ad.**
- **I understand what to do next.**

Then, as the visitor scrolls, each section should answer a new question rather than repeat the headline.

## Common mistakes

### Copying the ad instead of extending it

Repeating a one-line ad hook for eight sections does not create persuasion. Use the page to answer the next questions.

### Generic hero after a specific ad

If the ad says “easy morning routine” and the landing page says “Meet our award-winning wellness system,” the visitor has to reconnect the story themselves.

### New offer on the page

Do not add a discount, free gift, or shipping promise merely because it seems conversion-friendly. The page must match the real campaign setup.

### Creator voice disappears

For creator traffic, retain the relevant language and approved assets from the creator while keeping the page credible as a brand experience.

### Over-designing before validating the angle

Fix message match first. Fine typography is not a substitute for a coherent campaign.

## What to test next

Once the dedicated page is live, useful follow-up hypotheses include:

- benefit-led vs proof-led hero;
- creator-first vs product-first hero;
- proof before vs after education;
- shorter vs longer education for the same cold audience;
- different objection-handling order.

Use [Create a controlled variation](/academy/playbooks/controlled-variation) so the next test isolates the learning.
