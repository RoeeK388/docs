# Build a subscription campaign

Use this playbook when a subscription is an important part of the campaign and you want customers to understand **what they get, how often, what they save, and what commitment they are making** before they purchase.

The landing page can improve subscription framing. It cannot create a selling plan that does not exist in your commerce system.

## Before you build

Confirm the real plan terms:

```text
PRODUCT
[product]

ONE-TIME PRICE
[actual price]

SUBSCRIPTION PRICE / SAVINGS
[actual price or approved savings]

DELIVERY CADENCE(S)
[available selling plans]

SUBSCRIPTION TERMS
[approved skip / cancel / change information]

DEFAULT CAMPAIGN CHOICE
[one-time / subscription, if intentionally configured]

PRIMARY CUSTOMER REASON
[why subscription is useful to this audience]
```

Do not use a copied campaign brief as the source of truth when the live selling plan has changed.

## Step 1: decide what the campaign is actually testing or selling

There are several different subscription strategies:

- **subscription-first campaign** — the campaign deliberately leads with recurring purchase value
- **choice campaign** — one-time and subscription are equally legitimate choices
- **subscription education** — the page explains recurring value to reduce commitment concerns
- **subscription experiment** — you are testing presentation while keeping the underlying plan unchanged

Be clear which one you are building.

## Step 2: explain customer value before savings alone

“Save 15%” can help, but the customer may also care about:

- convenience
- replenishment cadence
- avoiding reordering
- flexibility, where the real terms support it
- access to a subscription-specific offer

Use only benefits supported by the actual program.

## Copy-ready Jurni prompt

```text
CONTEXT
This campaign is designed to help [audience] understand and choose between the real one-time and subscription options for [product].

REAL PLAN INFORMATION
One-time: [price / terms]
Subscription: [price / approved savings]
Cadence: [cadence]
Approved flexibility / terms: [terms]

GOAL
Make the choices easy to compare without making subscription feel hidden or misleading.

CHANGE / BUILD
- clearly label one-time and subscription choices
- explain the approved subscription value beside the choice
- show real price / savings dynamically where available
- address the main subscription objection: [objection]
- keep the purchase CTA close to the selected choice

KEEP
Use the existing real selling plans and product connection.
Do not create a new cadence, price, discount, or subscription term.

DO NOT
- imply “cancel anytime” unless it is approved and accurate
- invent savings
- make one-time look disabled if it is a valid option
- replace real selling-plan controls with static design elements
```

## Step 3: make the comparison answer the actual objection

Customers often hesitate because they do not understand the commitment.

A useful comparison answers:

- What do I pay now?
- How often will this arrive?
- What do I save?
- Can I change or stop it, if applicable?
- What happens if I choose one-time instead?

Do not overwhelm the choice with every policy detail. Put the most decision-relevant terms at the point of selection and link or explain additional terms appropriately.

## Step 4: keep the selected state obvious

The shopper should always know which purchase type is selected. Review:

- visual selected state
- price tied to the selection
- cadence / plan shown
- CTA follows the selection
- mobile tap targets

A card that looks selected but sends the one-time variant to cart is a critical QA failure.

## Step 5: place subscription proof near the concern

Good proof depends on the campaign. It might include approved customer feedback about routine/convenience, product usage context, or brand support information.

Do not create “most customers subscribe” or subscription-retention claims unless you actually have approved data.

## Step 6: QA the real selling plan

Test:

1. One-time selection.
2. Subscription selection.
3. Every cadence promoted by the page.
4. Variant changes while subscription is selected.
5. Final line item / selling plan in cart or checkout.
6. Displayed and charged price.
7. Discount code / offer combinations relevant to the campaign.
8. Back navigation when important to the flow.
9. Mobile.

The page can look correct while the wrong plan reaches checkout.

## Step 7: experiment with framing, not hidden economics

Useful tests include:

- convenience-led subscription explanation versus savings-led
- subscription card first versus one-time first, if the product configuration supports the intended default
- flexibility information beside selector versus FAQ
- price comparison emphasized versus product-benefit explanation emphasized

If you change the actual subscription discount at the same time, you are testing economics as well as presentation.

## Prompt: review subscription clarity

```text
Review the selected purchase section for subscription clarity.
Do not edit yet.

Using only the actual plan information supplied, answer:
1. Is one-time vs subscription obvious?
2. Is the selected choice obvious?
3. Are price and savings consistent?
4. Is cadence clear?
5. Are important commitment/flexibility terms clear?
6. Could any copy imply terms that are not guaranteed?
7. Is the choice easy to use on mobile?

Return BLOCKER / SHOULD FIX / OPTIONAL.
```

## What good looks like

A shopper should understand the recurring choice without needing to interpret tiny labels or discover the actual terms only in checkout.

The subscription value should feel connected to their use of the product—not just to a crossed-out price.

## Common mistakes

**Writing a subscription in the design before confirming the selling plan.** Visual copy does not create commerce behavior.

**Savings mismatch.** The page, product selector, cart, and checkout need to agree.

**Hiding one-time purchase.** If it exists as a valid option, don't make the experience misleading.

**Overpromising flexibility.** Use approved real terms.

**Static price in a dynamic selector.** Recheck every variant/plan combination.
