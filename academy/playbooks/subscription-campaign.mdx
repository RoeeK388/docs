Use this playbook when the campaign is intentionally designed to make **subscription the preferred purchase**, while still presenting the real purchase choices and terms accurately.

A subscription-first page should explain why recurring purchase fits the customer's use case. It should not create subscription value by inventing discounts, frequencies, cancellation rules, or flexibility that the real selling plan does not provide.

## Before you start

Confirm the real commerce setup:

- product;
- available selling plan(s);
- real subscription frequency / options;
- actual subscription price / savings;
- one-time price;
- approved cancellation / skip / pause language;
- subscription-provider behavior relevant to the customer;
- intended default selection in the page / purchase component.

Use [Subscriptions & Selling Plans](/building-funnels/subscriptions-selling-plans) for the current Jurni product setup.

## Phase 1 — Define why subscription matters for this audience

Good subscription positioning usually connects to a real customer job:

- routine / replenishment;
- convenience;
- approved savings;
- maintaining supply;
- product cadence that naturally repeats.

Write:

> Subscription is relevant for **[AUDIENCE]** because **[APPROVED CUSTOMER BENEFIT]**, and the actual plan offers **[REAL PLAN DETAILS]**.

If the only reason is “we want more subscribers,” the page needs a better customer rationale.

## Phase 2 — Make the commercial difference easy to understand

A shopper should be able to compare one-time and subscription without calculating it themselves.

Use the actual data supplied by the connected plan / approved source:

- price;
- savings, when real;
- frequency;
- approved flexibility / cancellation statement;
- what is included.

Do not type a promotional subscription price that does not match the real selling plan.

## Phase 3 — Brief the page around subscription value

```text
Adapt this campaign for a subscription-first purchase journey.

PRODUCT
[PRODUCT]

AUDIENCE
[AUDIENCE]

WHY SUBSCRIPTION FITS
[APPROVED CUSTOMER BENEFIT]

REAL PLAN DETAILS
- frequency / options: [DETAILS]
- approved savings: [SAVINGS]
- approved flexibility: [DETAILS]

PAGE CHANGES
- explain subscription value before the purchase decision;
- make the subscription option easy to compare with one-time;
- use approved copy around savings / flexibility;
- keep one-time available when it is part of the real purchase section;
- reinforce subscription only where it helps the customer's decision.

KEEP
Real product, selling plans, prices, quantities, and purchase action.

DO NOT INVENT
Selling plans, delivery frequency, savings, cancellation terms,
free shipping, gifts, or subscription benefits.
```

## Phase 4 — Decide how much subscription education is needed

For a familiar replenishment product, a short comparison may be enough.

For a new subscription model, you may need to explain:

- what arrives;
- how often;
- what happens after the first order;
- approved control / flexibility details;
- how savings work.

Do not repeat the same subscription benefit in the hero, three mid-page banners, every testimonial, and the FAQ. Use a clear hierarchy.

## Phase 5 — Use proof that actually supports subscription

Product reviews are not automatically subscription proof.

Useful subscription-specific proof might include approved customer quotes about:

- convenience;
- repeat routine;
- avoiding running out;
- value / savings;
- managing the plan.

Only use this if the customer actually said it. Do not rewrite a product-quality review into a subscription endorsement.

## Phase 6 — Make the purchase selector unmistakable

In the purchase section, check:

- which option is selected by default;
- visible price for each option;
- selling plan / frequency choices;
- discount / savings display;
- quantity;
- CTA action.

The visual hierarchy can prefer subscription while still letting the customer understand the alternative when it is available.

Do not turn plan cards into static artwork.

## Phase 7 — QA every relevant plan

Test the live purchase path:

1. Choose subscription.
2. Choose the intended frequency / plan.
3. Add to cart / checkout.
4. Confirm the line item carries the correct selling plan.
5. Confirm actual price / discount.
6. Return and test one-time purchase.
7. Test key variants if plans differ by variant.

If the page copy says “ships every 30 days,” confirm the selected selling plan actually does that.

## Phase 8 — Check other offers for compatibility

If subscription also qualifies for:

- GWP;
- free shipping;
- discount code;
- bundle offer;

verify the actual Shopify / commerce rule supports the combination. Do not assume an offer stacks because the page explains both.

## What good looks like

A shopper should be able to answer:

- Why would I subscribe?
- What will I pay?
- How often will it arrive?
- What choice do I have instead?
- What happens when I click?

The subscription should feel preferred because its value is clear, not because the one-time option is hidden or made artificially confusing.

## Common mistakes

### Typed savings that do not match the plan

Use real commerce data / approved offer.

### Subscription copy without real selling plan

A “Subscribe & Save” card is not a subscription implementation.

### Hiding one-time when it should remain available

Visual preference and actual choice are different decisions.

### Inventing flexibility language

Only state skip / pause / cancel terms your team has approved.

### Testing only the default plan

Verify the major frequencies / variants a customer can actually choose.

## Useful tests

- subscription-first vs balanced purchase framing;
- savings-led vs convenience-led subscription story;
- subscription education before vs inside the purchase section;
- default subscription selection vs one-time default only when both are legitimate product decisions.
