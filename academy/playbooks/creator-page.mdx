Use this playbook when traffic comes from a creator, influencer, UGC-style ad, affiliate, or founder/creator partnership and the landing page should continue that specific voice and use case.

The goal is **continuity without impersonation**. The page should feel like the natural next step after the creator content, while still being a clear brand-owned shopping experience.

## When to use it

Useful when:

- a creator ad has a distinctive hook or routine;
- a creator discount code drives campaign traffic;
- a creator's use case resonates with a specific audience;
- paid whitelisting / partnership content needs a dedicated destination;
- you want to compare a creator-matched page with a generic brand page.

## Before you start

Gather:

- the exact creator creative / script / caption;
- approved creator images / video stills;
- approved creator quote(s);
- the real affiliate / discount-code setup if relevant;
- connected product;
- audience and traffic source;
- approved product claims / proof;
- any disclosure requirements your team uses.

Do not ask Jurni to infer what the creator “probably thinks” about the product.

## Phase 1 — Identify what made the creator creative work

Ask Jurni to analyze the supplied creative:

```text
Do not build yet.
Analyze the attached creator creative for a landing-page brief.

Identify:
1. The audience being spoken to
2. The creator's core hook / angle
3. The use case or routine shown
4. Exact product / benefit emphasis
5. Tone and vocabulary
6. Proof or personal experience explicitly stated
7. Offer / code / CTA expectation
8. What the landing page still needs to explain

Separate what the creator actually says / shows from assumptions.
```

Review this with the campaign owner. The creator angle—not generic brand copy—should set the page direction.

## Phase 2 — Choose the creator's role on the page

Three useful patterns:

**Creator-led hero** — creator remains prominent in first screen, then the brand explains the product.

**Creator proof hero** — product / brand is primary, with creator quote / visual as the key proof.

**Creator editorial page** — creator's routine / experience becomes the page story, useful for longer education.

Choose based on the creative and brand relationship. Do not default to a giant creator portrait if product recognition suffers.

## Phase 3 — Build the matched page

```text
Build a landing page for traffic from the attached creator content.

CREATOR ANGLE
[ANGLE]

AUDIENCE
[AUDIENCE]

USE
- attached creator creative for voice, message, and approved creator content;
- connected product for real product / purchase information;
- approved creator assets only;
- AI Knowledge for brand claims / product facts;
- approved discount code / offer: [OFFER OR NONE].

FLOW
1. Hero that continues the creator's core angle
2. Creator proof / routine / approved quote
3. Product explanation tied to that use case
4. Benefits most relevant to this audience
5. Additional approved proof
6. Product / purchase section
7. FAQ for likely objections
8. Final CTA / offer reminder where relevant

Do not invent creator quotes, usage frequency, results, personal story,
discount codes, exclusivity, or endorsements.
Keep the experience clearly brand-owned.
```

## Phase 4 — Preserve the creator's language without copying noise

Creator content often works because it feels natural. The page can retain:

- customer language;
- routine / scenario;
- problem framing;
- specific product use;
- approved quote.

But remove platform-specific clutter that does not help the page:

- “link in bio”;
- comments / fake social UI;
- invented like counts;
- fake account chrome;
- overly casual text that makes product details ambiguous.

## Phase 5 — Keep proof authentic

If the creator said:

> “I keep this in my gym bag because…”

You can use that approved quote.

Do not turn it into:

> “Thousands of athletes rely on this every day.”

unless you have a separate approved source for that claim.

The creator is one source of proof, not permission to generalize their experience to everyone.

## Phase 6 — Coordinate discount / affiliate messaging

If the creator has a code:

- use the exact approved code;
- explain the actual offer;
- make the code easy to find near the purchase decision;
- verify the code works at checkout;
- confirm the affiliate / creator reference is configured as intended.

Do not assume that saving a creator record or reference automatically guarantees commission attribution. Verify the actual campaign path with the affiliate-program owner.

Use [Affiliate & creator tools](/integrations/affiliate-tools) for setup details.

## Phase 7 — Mobile-first creator experience

Creator traffic is often mobile. Check:

- creator visual does not bury headline / CTA;
- video / image ratio works on mobile;
- creator quote is readable without a huge text block;
- product appears early enough to avoid ambiguity;
- the discount code / primary action is easy to find;
- purchase selectors remain usable.

## Phase 8 — QA the transition from creator content

Open the real creator / ad link and ask:

- Does the page immediately continue the same message?
- Is the same product emphasized?
- Is the code / offer consistent?
- Does the creator content used on the page exactly match what was approved?
- Is the CTA expectation consistent?
- Does the purchase / checkout work?
- Are campaign / affiliate parameters preserved as intended?

## What good looks like

The visitor should feel:

> “This is the product / offer the creator was talking about, I understand why they use it, and now the brand is giving me the information I need to decide.”

If the page feels like a generic PDP after a highly specific creator ad, the match is weak. If it looks like the creator secretly owns the whole website, the brand / disclosure boundary may be too blurry.

## Common mistakes

### Inventing creator voice

Use actual source material. Do not fabricate quotes because they sound authentic.

### Overusing creator content

The creator gets the visitor interested; the product still needs enough clear brand information to support a purchase.

### Code mismatch

A visible code that fails in checkout creates immediate trust and support issues.

### Product buried below creator story

Show what the creator is talking about early.

### Fake social interface

Do not add fake likes, comments, usernames, or platform UI as proof.

## Useful tests

- creator-first vs product-first hero;
- creator quote vs creator image as primary proof;
- creator matched page vs generic PDP;
- code-led vs product-led hero when the same real offer applies;
- short routine page vs longer creator story.
