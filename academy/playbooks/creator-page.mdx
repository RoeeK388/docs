# Build a creator or influencer landing page

Use this playbook when traffic comes from a specific creator and the creator's **voice, use case, or recommendation angle** is part of why the visitor clicked.

A strong creator page keeps the first part of the experience familiar, then hands the visitor smoothly from **creator trust → product understanding → brand proof → purchase**.

## Before you build

Collect the creator material you are allowed to use:

- ad creative or organic post
- approved transcript
- creator images or video
- exact approved quotes
- creator-specific offer or code, when applicable
- connected product
- campaign destination and tracking plan

Do not ask AI to infer what the creator “would say.” Use only approved creator material.

## Step 1: identify the real creator angle

Fill in:

```text
CREATOR
[name / handle]

AUDIENCE EXPECTATION
[why their audience trusts them for this topic]

HOOK
[what they said that earned the click]

PRODUCT REASON
[why they personally recommend / use it, if approved]

APPROVED QUOTES
[exact wording]

OFFER / CODE
[approved details]

PRIMARY ACTION
[action]
```

The creator's fame is not the page strategy. Their **specific reason for recommending the product** is usually more important.

## Step 2: keep the transition recognizable

The first screen can use:

- creator image or video from the campaign
- the same or closely related hook
- an exact creator quote
- a short bridge to the product
- the creator-specific approved offer

Do not immediately replace the creator's language with generic “premium quality” brand copy.

## Copy-ready Jurni prompt

```text
Build a landing page for traffic from [creator].

SOURCES
Use the attached creator campaign as the source for creator voice, imagery, and approved quotes.
Use the connected product and AI Knowledge for product and brand information.

HERO
Continue this creator angle: [angle].
Use an approved creator image / video and this exact quote where appropriate: [quote].
Make the product and primary CTA obvious.

PAGE FLOW
1. Creator-matched hero.
2. Short section explaining the creator's approved product reason / use case.
3. Brand-owned product education.
4. Approved customer / product proof.
5. Product / purchase section with [offer / code if applicable].
6. FAQ for campaign objections.

VOICE
Keep creator material distinct from brand-owned explanation. Do not make brand copy look like a creator quote.

DO NOT
- invent creator quotes or endorsements
- imply the creator made claims they did not make
- change the approved offer
- invent review data or product results
```

## Step 3: mark ownership of the message

Visitors should be able to distinguish:

- **creator quote or testimonial** — exact approved creator words
- **brand explanation** — your product education
- **customer reviews** — other approved customer proof

Do not put quotation marks around AI-written brand copy and visually attribute it to the creator.

## Step 4: make creator-specific proof useful

The creator's experience is strongest when it supports a relevant customer question.

Examples:

- creator demonstrates how they use the product → routine / usability
- creator explains why they switched → comparison consideration, if approved
- creator shows product format → product understanding
- creator names their favorite option → choice simplification, if approved

Do not stretch a casual “I love this” into a clinical or performance claim.

## Step 5: transition into brand proof

Creator trust can earn attention, but the visitor may still need product details the creator did not cover.

After the creator-led opening, answer:

- what exactly is the product?
- why does it fit the promised use case?
- what approved proof supports it?
- what are the purchase choices?
- what questions could still stop purchase?

This transition prevents the page from becoming one long endorsement.

## Step 6: handle creator offers correctly

If the campaign uses a code or affiliate reference, verify the real setup separately from the visual page.

The page can show approved offer copy, but final pricing and eligibility depend on the commerce system. Affiliate references and creator discount codes are also different pieces of the campaign.

Use the Help Center for exact [affiliate setup](/integrations/affiliate-tools) and [discount configuration](/commerce/discount-code-offers).

## Step 7: review for over-attribution

```text
Review this creator landing page.
Do not edit yet.

Flag anything that makes it look like the creator said, endorsed, or personally experienced something that is not present in the supplied creator material.

Separate:
- exact creator content
- brand-owned copy
- customer proof
- product facts

Also flag any place where the creator angle disappears too quickly after the hero.
```

## What good looks like

The page should feel like a natural continuation of the creator content for the first several screens, while gradually giving the brand enough space to answer the purchase questions the creator did not.

It should be clear what the creator actually said and what the brand is explaining.

## Common mistakes

**Using the creator as decoration.** A creator photo beside generic brand copy does not preserve the reason for the click.

**Inventing creator voice.** Never fabricate quotes or first-person experiences.

**Staying creator-led for too long.** The brand still needs to explain the product and purchase.

**Offer inconsistency.** Check the code, price, gift, and actual checkout behavior.

**One generic page for every creator.** If creators sell the product for materially different reasons, separate experiences can preserve congruence better.

## What to test next

- creator quote in hero versus creator visual with brand headline
- creator proof above product versus after product education
- creator-specific page versus best generic control
- creator angle maintained through two sections versus hero only
