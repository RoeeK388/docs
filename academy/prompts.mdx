Use this library when you know the job you want Jurni to do and do not want to start from a blank prompt.

Every prompt is designed to be **adapted**, not pasted blindly. Replace the bracketed fields, attach the real sources, and remove instructions that do not apply to your campaign.

If you want to understand *why* these prompts are structured this way, read [Prompting 101](/academy/prompting-101) first.

## Five prompts worth saving today

### 1. Turn a rough idea into a proper brief

```text
Do not build yet.
Act like a senior performance marketer reviewing this campaign idea.

My rough idea:
[PASTE IDEA]

Identify only the missing information that would materially change the page:
- audience;
- traffic source / ad angle;
- product / offer;
- primary action;
- proof we can use;
- must-keep brand or commerce behavior.

Ask me the smallest number of questions needed.
Then rewrite the final brief in the Jurni BRIEF format.
```

### 2. Build a message-matched landing page

```text
Build a dedicated landing page for [PRODUCT] for [AUDIENCE].
Traffic is coming from [CHANNEL / AD].
The campaign angle is [ANGLE].

Use:
- attached product for real product information;
- attached ad for message and visual direction;
- AI Knowledge for approved brand voice and claims;
- supplied reviews / UGC only for social proof.

The first screen should clearly continue the ad's story.
Use one primary conversion path.
Do not invent pricing, discounts, claims, reviews, urgency, or guarantees.
Prioritize mobile.
```

### 3. Make one section better without touching the rest

```text
Edit only the selected [SECTION].
The problem is: [SPECIFIC PROBLEM].
Desired result: [SPECIFIC OUTCOME].

Keep unchanged:
- all other sections;
- existing copy that is not explicitly mentioned;
- product and purchase behavior;
- CTA destination;
- desktop/mobile behavior outside this section.
```

### 4. Ask Jurni for a CRO audit without fake certainty

```text
Do not edit yet.
Audit this page for [AUDIENCE] arriving from [TRAFFIC SOURCE].

For each finding, label it:
- PAGE OBSERVATION — directly visible;
- HYPOTHESIS — a testable interpretation;
- DATA NEEDED — something we should confirm in analytics, research, cart, or checkout.

Prioritize the 5 highest-leverage issues.
Do not invent performance data or customer research.
```

### 5. Create a clean A/B-test variation

```text
This is an experiment variant.
Change only: [HYPOTHESIS].

Keep the following identical to control:
- product and purchase behavior;
- offer and price;
- all non-target sections;
- CTA destination;
- tracking setup;
- responsive behavior unless it is part of the hypothesis.

After editing, summarize exactly what changed compared with control.
```

## How to use this library

A good workflow is:

1. Copy the closest prompt.
2. Replace the placeholders with the real campaign context.
3. Attach the source material you want Jurni to trust.
4. Delete irrelevant instructions.
5. Send the prompt.
6. Review the result and continue with smaller follow-ups.
7. Save a prompt that worked well for your team as an internal reusable template.

<Tip>
  The best reusable prompts capture **decisions and constraints**, not a fixed visual template. Keep the structure repeatable while allowing the product, audience, ad angle, evidence, and offer to change.
</Tip>
