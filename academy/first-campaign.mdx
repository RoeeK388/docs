This playbook is the shortest path from **“we have an idea”** to **“traffic is hitting a verified Jurni campaign.”** It is designed for a marketer launching a new landing page, not for someone configuring every Jurni feature at once.

## The outcome

By the end, you should have:

- one campaign brief with a clear audience, angle, offer, and action;
- one reviewed Jurni experience;
- the correct product / purchase path for the campaign;
- the publishing destination you intend to use;
- a campaign-facing Smart Link when appropriate;
- tracking checked on the real URL;
- one documented hypothesis for what you will learn after launch.

## 1. Write the campaign brief before opening the editor

Do not begin with “build me a landing page.” Give Jurni a point of view.

```text
CAMPAIGN
Product: [product]
Audience: [who is this for?]
Traffic source: [Meta / TikTok / Google / email / creator / other]
Audience temperature: [cold / warm / returning]
Primary angle: [the message from the ad or campaign]
Offer: [approved offer, or “no special offer”]
Primary action: [add to cart / choose subscription / continue to store / sign up]

PROOF WE CAN USE
- [approved benefit or claim]
- [review / rating / UGC source]
- [shipping / guarantee / trust detail]

MUST KEEP
- [brand or legal requirement]
- [purchase behavior]

DO NOT INVENT
Claims, customer results, reviews, prices, discounts, deadlines, or guarantees.
```

If the brief is unclear, use the [Prompting 101](/academy/prompting-101) meta-prompt to ask Jurni what information is missing before it builds.

## 2. Gather only the references that matter

Attach the actual inputs Jurni should work from: the connected product when available, the ad creative, approved copy or claims, product imagery, and any reference URL, screenshot, or Figma frame that genuinely matters.

Label references. A screenshot can mean **“copy this layout”** or **“place this image in the page.”** Those are different instructions.

```text
Reference 1 = layout inspiration only.
Reference 2 = actual product image to use.
Attached ad = source of campaign angle and headline direction.
Attached product = source of product and purchase information.
```

## 3. Build the first useful version

Start with structure and message match, not micro-styling.

```text
Build a landing page for the campaign brief above.

The first screen should immediately connect to the ad angle and make the next action obvious.
Use a clear visual hierarchy and keep one primary conversion path.

Recommended flow:
1. Hero that matches the campaign message
2. Fast proof / trust
3. Product benefits or problem-solution education
4. Social proof using only approved sources
5. Product / purchase section
6. Objection handling / FAQ
7. Final CTA

Use our brand guidance and attached assets.
Do not invent proof or commerce details.
Prioritize mobile because [X]% of this campaign's traffic is expected to be mobile.
```

Review the page structure before asking for ten additional changes. If the story is wrong, fix the story first.

## 4. Refine the highest-leverage parts

Work section by section. Usually the order is hero / above the fold, proof, purchase section, mobile, then long-form education and FAQ.

Use [Prompting 101](/academy/prompting-101) to preserve working content while editing one area.

## 5. Confirm the purchase path

For Shopify campaigns, test the actual product, variant, selling plan, quantity, discount, cart, and checkout that the page is supposed to use.

For non-Shopify or headless flows, confirm the agreed cart API, checkout link, product-page destination, or custom handoff before launch. See [Cart & Checkout](/commerce/cart-checkout-behavior).

The page is not finished because the CTA looks correct. It is finished when the intended shopper action works.

## 6. Choose how the page goes live

Use the [Publishing overview](/publishing/overview) if you need setup details. From a campaign perspective, choose based on the destination you need: Jurni-hosted, Shopify Pages, or the agreed headless/custom route.

If paid media, email, creators, or QR codes should use a stable campaign-facing URL, create or use a [Smart Link](/building-funnels/smart-links).

## 7. Test the exact link you will send traffic to

Never QA only the editor preview. Open the final campaign link and verify destination, mobile and desktop, purchase path, offer outcome, forms/reviews, consent, tracking, campaign parameters, and checkout transition.

Use [Check your campaign link](/publishing/check-campaign-link) for the operational checklist.

## 8. Define what you want to learn

A launch should create a learning loop even when it is not a formal A/B test.

> We believe [specific page/campaign choice] will help [audience] because [reason]. We will watch [primary metric] and [secondary diagnostic].

If you can isolate the change, turn it into an experiment with [Run your first experiment](/academy/playbooks/first-experiment).

## What good looks like

Before launching, a teammate who did not build the page should be able to answer: Who is this page for? What brought them here? What is the main promise? What should they do next? What proof supports it? What are they actually buying?

If those answers are fuzzy, another round of styling is usually not the highest-priority fix.
