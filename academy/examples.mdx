Use this page when you know **the campaign situation** but are not sure which Jurni workflow or page type fits best.

These are **campaign archetypes, not customer case studies**. They show how to think about the job without inventing performance results or pretending one page type is universally best.

## Cold Meta traffic from a problem-led ad

**Situation:** The ad calls out a specific frustration. The current destination is a generic PDP.

**Good starting point:** [Ad → landing page](/academy/playbooks/ad-to-landing-page)

**Page direction:**
- hero continues the same problem / promise;
- quick product recognition;
- proof connected to the specific concern;
- enough product education for a cold visitor;
- clear purchase section.

**Useful prompt:** [Paid-social landing-page prompt](/academy/prompts/page-building)

**Follow-up test:** benefit-led vs proof-led hero.

## Creator ad with a strong routine angle

**Situation:** The creator explains how the product fits their morning / gym / travel / night routine.

**Good starting point:** [Creator / UGC page](/academy/playbooks/creator-page)

**Page direction:**
- creator angle in the first screen;
- approved creator quote / asset;
- product explanation tied to that routine;
- brand proof and product detail after the creator hook;
- creator code / offer only when real.

**Common failure:** sending the traffic to a brand-generic page that loses the creator context after the click.

---

## New category that needs education

**Situation:** Visitors understand the problem but not why this type of product is relevant.

**Good starting points:**
- [Advertorial](/academy/playbooks/advertorial) when the insight / mechanism needs explanation;
- [Listicle](/academy/playbooks/listicle) when the education naturally breaks into criteria / reasons.

**Page direction:** educate before the purchase pitch, then use the product as the logical implementation of the approved criteria.

**Common failure:** adding more product benefits before the customer understands the category.

---

## New product from an established brand

**Situation:** Existing customers trust the brand but need to understand why the new product matters.

**Good starting point:** [Product launch page](/academy/playbooks/product-launch)

**Page direction:**
- customer relevance first;
- what is genuinely new;
- approved product differentiation;
- existing brand trust where it is relevant;
- real launch proof without fabricating new-product reviews.

**Common failure:** “New!” becomes the entire value proposition.

---

## Founder-led brand with a credible origin story

**Situation:** The founder’s connection to the problem explains why the product was built the way it was.

**Good starting point:** [Founder story](/academy/playbooks/founder-story)

**Page direction:** customer problem → founder insight → product principle → proof → purchase.

**Common failure:** the page becomes a biography instead of helping the shopper decide.

---

## Search / high-intent traffic

**Situation:** Visitors already know the category and are evaluating what to buy.

**Likely direction:** a more direct product / comparison journey rather than a long problem-awareness story.

Focus on:
- product recognition;
- key differentiation;
- purchase options;
- proof;
- comparison criteria;
- FAQ / objections.

Use [CRO audit](/academy/playbooks/cro-audit) on the existing page before assuming a new long-form format is needed.

---

## Retargeting visitors who already saw the brand

**Situation:** The audience has already visited / engaged and may not need basic category education again.

**Prompt starting point:** [Retargeting-page prompt](/academy/prompts/campaigns-offers)

Potential page priorities:
- strongest proof;
- objection handling;
- approved offer;
- purchase clarity;
- product differentiation.

**Common failure:** repeating the same top-of-funnel education as the first visit.

---

## Gift-with-purchase promotion

**Situation:** The incentive is a gift after a spend / product / subscription condition.

**Good starting point:** [GWP campaign](/academy/playbooks/gwp-campaign)

Think in three layers:
1. campaign message;
2. Jurni gift qualification / supported selection behavior;
3. actual Shopify gift pricing / discount.

**Common failure:** “free gift” copy is launched before the actual checkout rule makes the gift free.

---

## Subscription acquisition campaign

**Situation:** The brand wants to lead with subscription because recurring purchase genuinely fits the product / customer routine.

**Good starting point:** [Subscription-first campaign](/academy/playbooks/subscription-campaign)

Focus on:
- real subscription value;
- accurate frequency / savings;
- clear comparison with one-time when available;
- live selling-plan QA.

**Common failure:** using static “Subscribe & Save” design without matching the real selling plan.

---

## “The page feels weak” but the team has no hypothesis

**Situation:** Feedback is subjective and there are too many possible changes.

**Good starting point:** [CRO audit → first edit](/academy/playbooks/cro-audit)

Ask AI to separate:
- visible observation;
- hypothesis;
- data needed.

Then choose one high-leverage customer problem instead of rebuilding everything.

---

## A strong page exists, but the team wants to improve it

**Situation:** You do not want to throw away the current control.

**Good starting points:**
- [Optimize above the fold](/academy/playbooks/optimize-above-fold)
- [Controlled variation](/academy/playbooks/controlled-variation)
- [First experiment](/academy/playbooks/first-experiment)

**Common failure:** “make a better version” changes five unrelated things and teaches the team very little.

---

## A competitor / inspiration page has a great interaction

**Situation:** You want the pattern, not their brand.

**Good starting point:** [Build from a reference](/academy/playbooks/reference-page)

Tell Jurni separately:
- what to use for layout;
- what to use for interaction;
- what content / commerce comes from your own sources.

**Common failure:** treating the reference as source material for claims, reviews, prices, or product behavior.

## Choose by customer awareness, not trend

A useful rule of thumb:

| Visitor situation | Page usually needs more of… |
| --- | --- |
| Cold / problem-aware | context, education, proof, message match |
| Product-aware | differentiation, proof, purchase clarity |
| High intent | decision support, product / offer, objections |
| Returning / retargeting | proof, reason to act, purchase clarity |

This is a planning heuristic, not a rigid funnel model. Your creative, product, category, and evidence should shape the actual page.
