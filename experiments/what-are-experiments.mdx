Use a Jurni **experiment** when you have a specific question about which page experience works better for the same campaign traffic.

A simple way to think about it is:

**Your ad or campaign uses one Smart Link → Jurni assigns incoming visitors to the experiment variants → you compare what those visitors do.**

The experiment is the testing layer behind the campaign URL. You do not need to create separate ad destinations for each Jurni variant.

## A real example

Suppose your Meta ad leads with **convenience**, but the current landing page hero leads with a broad brand message.

Your hypothesis might be:

> If the hero continues the convenience angle, first-time Meta visitors will be more likely to purchase because the page will feel immediately relevant to the ad they clicked.

You keep the product, offer, CTA, and rest of the page the same, then compare:

- **Control:** current hero
- **Variant:** convenience-focused hero

That is a cleaner experiment than redesigning the whole page and calling the result a “hero test.”

## How a Jurni experiment works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with a question">
    Write the observation, hypothesis, one intended change, and the primary metric you will use to judge it. If you need help choosing the test, use the [Jurni Academy CRO review](/academy/playbooks/cro-review).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the variants">
    Build and publish the pages you want to compare. A variant can use a Jurni page or a supported existing website destination when the required measurement is connected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the Smart Link">
    Create a new Smart Link for a new campaign, or deliberately connect an existing Smart Link when the campaign is already sending traffic through it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the traffic split">
    Add the variant destinations and choose the intended weights. The percentages describe the intended allocation over traffic; they are not a promise that every small sample will be perfectly even.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send campaign traffic through the Smart Link">
    Use the experiment's Smart Link as the campaign entry URL. Going directly to an individual variant bypasses the normal experiment entry path.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the result against the original hypothesis">
    Use the same date range and compare the primary metric you selected before looking at the result. Use other metrics to understand behavior, not to quietly replace the success question after the test starts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Use [Create an experiment](/experiments/create-experiment) for the exact setup controls or [Run your first A/B test](/academy/playbooks/first-ab-test) for the complete marketer workflow.

## New Smart Link or existing Smart Link?

**Create a new Smart Link** when you are launching a new campaign and want a fresh campaign URL.

**Use an existing Smart Link** when a live campaign already sends traffic through that link and you intentionally want the experiment to begin routing that traffic across variants.

Before connecting a live link, confirm what currently receives its traffic. The Smart Link is not just a label in the experiment—it is the campaign entry URL.

## What should I test?

Good experiments change one meaningful customer decision, for example:

- campaign-specific hero message
- proof placement
- shorter versus longer education path
- product introduced earlier versus later
- offer presentation
- clearer one-time versus subscription framing
- comparison section versus no comparison section

A complete redesign can still be compared, but it answers a broader question: **“Did this whole treatment perform differently?”** It tells you less about which individual change mattered.

See [Decide what to test next](/academy/playbooks/what-to-test) for a prioritization framework.

## Which metric should I care about?

Choose the metric that matches the hypothesis.

| If your question is mainly… | A useful primary metric may be… |
| --- | --- |
| “Did more sessions become orders?” | **CVR** |
| “Did this treatment create more revenue per visit?” | **RPS** |
| “Did purchasers build larger baskets?” | **AOV** |
| “Did more visitors interact with the product / CTA?” | **CTR / Add-to-Cart / Checkout Rate**, depending on the step |

Jurni also shows Sessions, Orders, and Revenue so you can understand the scale behind the rate metrics.

Do not pick whichever metric looks best after the result. See [Reading experiment results](/experiments/reading-results) for the current reporting workflow and [Metrics glossary](/data-dictionary/metrics-glossary) for definitions.

## Why can the traffic split look uneven?

Weights are the intended allocation over traffic. Small samples can naturally look uneven, and returning visitors can retain their existing assignment. Repeatedly refreshing your own browser is not a useful way to validate the split.

Use [How traffic assignment works](/experiments/traffic-assignment) when you need the detailed behavior.

## When should I use Routing Rules?

Routing Rules are for traffic that should go to a specific variant based on URL parameters rather than simply joining the normal weighted comparison.

For example, you might intentionally send one campaign parameter to a matching experience. That is useful routing behavior, but it changes how that traffic participates in the experiment. See [Routing Rules](/experiments/routing-rules).

## Before you launch

Open the exact Smart Link and verify:

- the intended destination variants are published
- mobile works
- product / CTA / cart behavior is correct in every variant
- campaign parameters behave as intended
- required tracking is connected

If you are testing an external website page, a link opening successfully does not mean Jurni can measure all of the outcomes you need. Follow [Testing a Jurni page against an existing website](/experiments/testing-against-website).

## Pausing and ending need deliberate traffic decisions

<Warning>
  The current pause flow warns that traffic will go to the shop home page. Ending an experiment asks you to choose the winning destination that should continue receiving traffic. Do not pause or end a live campaign casually—review [Pause, end, and reuse an experiment](/experiments/pause-end-reuse) first.
</Warning>
