A **product** is the item your brand sells. A **Buy Box** is the purchasing interface on a page: it presents the product, lets shoppers make the supported choices, and sends that selection to cart, checkout, or another destination.

The catalog, the product's supporting content, the Buy Box design, and the final purchase are connected—but changing one does not automatically change all the others.

## Understand the four parts

| Part | What you manage | Where to work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Connected product catalog** | The real products, variants, prices, availability, and selling plans exposed by your commerce connection. | Your commerce platform and Jurni's Products area. |
| **Jurni product content** | Supporting benefits, details, reviews, media, and other product customization used by compatible components. | **Products → Edit Product Data**. This content can be shared across experiences using the product. |
| **Page and Buy Box** | The layout, selected product, visible controls, content placement, and purchasing action. | The Jurni editor. |
| **Cart and checkout** | The actual items, plan, discount, and amount the shopper purchases. | The connected commerce flow, tested from the published page. |

For example, changing the headline above a Buy Box does not change the selected product. Editing a product's shared benefits is broader than changing a paragraph on one page. Showing “20% off” in a design does not create that discount in checkout.

## Build the purchasing flow in order

First confirm the product exists and is usable through the connected store. Next add the Buy Box or approved reusable section. Select the intended product and configure the purchasing behavior supported by that section. Then style it, review both device views, and test the actual cart and checkout.

Different Buy Boxes can expose different controls. A standard library template, an older purchase-options component, and a custom AI-built purchasing section do not necessarily have the same configuration panel. Use the controls and behavior of the section you actually placed.

## When to use a Buy Box

Use a Buy Box when shoppers need to choose a product configuration on the page—for example, a size, flavor, or one-time versus subscription purchase. A simple CTA can be enough when the page only needs to link to an existing product page or another destination.

A subscription or bundle design needs its actual purchase logic connected; it is not complete just because the selection cards look correct.

## Know the scope before editing

A local section belongs to its page. A Saved Section becomes an independent copy when inserted. A Global Section references a shared source. Product data edited from **Products** is also shared product information, not automatically a local change to one page.

Use [Product editing scope](/products/editing-scope), [Saved Sections](/building-funnels/saved-sections), and [Global Sections](/build/global-sections) before reusing a complex purchasing area.

## Platform and publishing differences

Shopify provides the native commerce connection. Headless and non-Shopify stores use the product/cart integration confirmed for their stack. A Shopify-specific selling-plan or cart instruction should not be assumed to work unchanged on another provider.

A Buy Box on a Jurni-hosted page and one published through native Shopify Pages must each be tested in their final environment. See [Cart and checkout behavior](/commerce/cart-checkout-behavior) and [Shopify Pages cart configuration](/shopify-pages/cart-config).
